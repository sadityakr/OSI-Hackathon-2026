# What to ask about the measurement, and why

Ask for what is missing when a user is present; otherwise state the assumption under limits. Ask only the
questions that change the conclusion at hand.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Laser wavelength | Graphene D and 2D disperse (about 50 and 100 cm-1/eV; Ferrari and Basko, Nat. Nanotechnol. 8, 235, 2013), so 2D positions are only comparable at one laser; the Cancado defect relation carries a lambda^4 factor; TMD resonances (2LA(M) in WS2) depend on it; CrSBr Ag2 polarization rotates with excitation energy (Mondal et al., npj 2D Mater. 2025, arXiv 2410.22164); Lee et al. 2012 strain/doping vectors are calibrated at 514.5 nm only. |
| Laser power and spot size | Heating shifts and broadens lines; damage creates defects (D band in graphene, LA(M) in MoS2). |
| Objective and NA | Spot size, hence power density; high NA mixes polarizations. |
| Grating, resolution, calibration | Needed to resolve 2LA(M) from E2g in WS2 (about 4 cm-1 apart) and to trust 1 to 3 cm-1 layer and strain shifts. |
| Polarization configuration | Selection rules: A1g vanishes in crossed polarization in hexagonal crystals; anisotropic crystals (black phosphorus, CrSBr, ReS2) need the angle to the crystal axes. |
| Substrate and oxide thickness | Interference changes intensity ratios; substrates strain and dope the flake; Si lines must be assigned. |
| Temperature and magnetic field | Phonon shifts; magnetic order in CrSBr and CrI3 adds or changes Raman features (CrI3: A1g appears in crossed polarization in the ordered state, Zhang et al., Nano Lett. 2020). |
| Low-frequency filter cutoff | Whether shear and breathing modes (layer counting) can be seen at all. |
| Sample history | Exfoliated, CVD, transferred, irradiated, annealed, encapsulated, aged in air: sets the prior on defects, strain, doping and oxidation. |
