The faint optical image agrees with a thin flake but is not specific to one layer.
- The Lorentzian shape assumption and the ALS baseline in the 900-1000 cm-1 region where Si second-order bands overlap can bias the width of the flake-2 E2g slightly; the 1474 cm-1 'peak' is noise.
- Data start at 450 cm-1, so the low-frequency interlayer shear mode (about 50 cm-1 in bulk) is not accessible; its absence is not evidence. For a monolayer, the shear mode would be absent by definition, and its observation (with its N-dependent frequency) would be the cleanest Raman layer counter, but this needs a low-frequency setup.

*Questions for the user:*
- What is the thickness of flake 1 (AFM or contrast), and has either flake been AFM-profiled? Without it, the intensity ratio cannot be turned into a layer number.
- Did you record an optical contrast (green channel at about 530 nm) for flake 2? On 290 nm SiO2 the contrast per layer is small, about 1-2.5 %, and it changes sign near 530 nm, so it should discriminate 1L from 2L when compared with a known flake.
- Is flake 2 laterally larger than the laser spot, and was the spot fully on the flake? Edge or partial coverage lowers the intensity without changing N.
- Was either sample annealed or transferred (e.g. PDMS), and what is the source crystal and isotope composition? Annealing upshifts thin hBN by about 3 cm-1 (Cai 2017), and isotope content changes the width floor.
- What are the spectrometer resolution, the integration time and the power? The same settings were stated, but absolute counts are needed to compare with literature intensity-vs-N curves.
- Can you provide a bare-Si spectrum under the same conditions? The Si line intensity ratio then gives a transmission-like cue for thickness.
- Is a low-frequency range (below 100 cm-1) or a 325/266 nm UV measurement available? A shear-mode check or UV excitation would test the layer count independently of the E2g position.
