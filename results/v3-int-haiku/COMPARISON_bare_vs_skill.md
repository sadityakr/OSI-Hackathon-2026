*Caveats:*
- Layer number cannot be read from the E2g position: Cai 2017 finds substrate-bound 1L, 2L and 3L hBN at 1369.6, 1369.0 and 1367.5 cm-1 respectively (within error bars of each other), and attributes the shift mainly to substrate strain. The +2.85 cm-1 upshift is therefore strain-or-thickness, not a layer count.
- The 18x change in the Si line between the two spectra is larger than any absorption a 1 to 2 layer hBN film could produce at 514.5 nm. The two acquisitions therefore differ in focus, spot placement or coverage, which can change the absolute E2g/Si ratio. Only ratios within one spectrum (E2g/Si second order, about 1/226) and the relative E2g shift are robust.
- The 1/190 to 1/226 intensity ratio needs flake 1's thickness to become a layer count. If the intensity scales linearly with N, flake 1 would have to be about 190 to 226 times thicker than flake 2 (for flake 2 = 1 L). That is possible but unverified; I have not assumed it.
- Optical contrast: the note's contrast values (about 2.5 % per layer) are for about 80 nm SiO2. This stack is 290 nm SiO2, where the contrast differs and changes sign with wavelength, so 'barely visible' only weakly supports a thin flake.
- Fit-only errors: the +2.85 cm-1 shift uncertainty of 0.25 cm-1 is the covariance error of a single fit. Lab-to-lab calibration scatter is several tenths of a cm-1, but the two Si offsets (0.21 and 0.20) agree to 0.01 cm-1 in one session, so the differential shift is more robust than the absolute position.
- The fit residual of flake 2 (rl_fit) has a systematic negative dip of about -200 counts at 450 to 600 cm-1. This is the Lorentzian Si line not matching its wings (or a baseline effect). It is far from 1369 cm-1 and does not change the E2g parameters, but it shows the Si line is not a clean Lorentzian.
- Widths: the two conflicting bulk FWHM values in the reference note (9 cm-1 from Gorbachev's HWHM and from Purdie's FWHM) mean the flake 1 width (15.5 cm-1) cannot be judged as bulk-like or not from the notes. Instrument resolution is bounded by the Si line (about 3.5 cm-1) but was not measured directly.
- Single spectrum per flake, one spot each. Spot-to-spot variation within a flake is known to be significant (Gorbachev 2011, p.4), so one spectrum per flake is not enough to characterise a flake.
- The E2g line at 1369 cm-1 in flake 2 is 19 cm-1 from graphene D at 514.5 nm (about 1350 cm-1). Graphene is not expected here, but it is not excluded by the spectrum alone if the spot overlaps a graphene flake.

*Questions for the user:*
- What is the AFM or profilometer thickness of flake 1 and of flake 2? Without flake 1's thickness, the E2g intensity cannot be turned into a layer count, and an AFM step on flake 2 would settle the monolayer question directly.
- Why does the Si line differ by 18x between the two spectra (about 18000 vs 980 counts) at the same power and integration? Was the focus, spot position or flake coverage different? Was the flake 2 spot entirely on hBN?
- Was either flake annealed, or is it an as-exfoliated flake? Annealing shifts 1L hBN upward by about 3 cm-1 (Cai 2017), which is the size of the observed shift.
- What were the objective NA, laser power at the sample, spot size and spectrometer resolution? The resolution limits how far the hBN widths can be interpreted.
- Do you have a second spot on flake 2 and on flake 1, or a Raman map? A position histogram over several spots would show whether the +2.85 cm-1 shift is a flake-wide property or one spot.
- Is there a known monolayer reference on the same SiO2 thickness (290 nm) that could be measured in the same session? That would calibrate the intensity and contrast directly.
