## Judging criteria (from Slack, 2026-10-18)

1. **Potential for impact:** could this change how research is done? Better results, faster progress, or easier work for researchers or learners?
2. **Innovativeness:** compared with the obvious solution, how creative is the approach? Unexpected combination of methods, data or interfaces, or reframing the problem?
3. **Relevance to the physical sciences and math:** how directly does it address real problems in physics, chemistry, materials science, astronomy, mathematics or related?
4. **Novelty:** compared with existing tools, published work and prior projects, does it deliver a capability that did not exist before? Does it open avenues others could build on?

## Submission requirements

1. A **public social media post or thread with a video** (max 4 minutes, no speed-up of voice). Any platform without a login wall.
2. **Up to one page write-up with a figure.**
3. **Link to code and/or datasets** developed.
4. Submission form (link expected in the kickoff communications).

Models and harnesses (Fable, Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon) are much stronger than last year. Scope ambitiously; ask whether "two days to a real discovery" is now possible. Speed over polish, learning over perfection, collaboration over silos.
