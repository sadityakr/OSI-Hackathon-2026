   With the results presented in Fig. 1(a), we recall the temperature evolution of the Raman scattering response typically observed in FePS3 crystals [6,8,24,36,37]. The Fi ( i = 1 , 2 , 3 , 4) features observed above 200 cm -1 are largely due to molecular-like vibration of the (P2S6) 4 -anion unit (which together with two Fe 2 + cations form the simplified unit cell of FePS3) and are pretty common for all TMPT compounds [38,39]. Instead, the Raman scattering peaks seen at lower energies ( Pi features) are expected to be due to phonons which include the vibration of Fe 2 + ions [8,38] while the M feature is now well recognized [24] as due to the magnon-gap excitation. As previously reported [6,8,24] and illustrated in Fig. 1(a), the magnon peak M as well as all Pi peaks are sensitive to the magnetic ordering: when temperature is raised above the Néel temperature of TN ≈ 120 K, the intensity of the P 4 resonance drops down abruptly, whereas Pi ( i = 1,2,3) and M peaks merge together into a broad P 0 feature. The P 4 peak is commonly associated to a Ag / Bg phonon from the center of the Brillouin zone of the FePS3 crystal [8,24,37,38,40,41]. Instead, the identification of phonons associated with Pi ( i = 0,1,2,3) resonances is less conclusive and we will comment more on this issue later on.

   In summary, we have systematically investigated the magnetic and structural evolution of MnPS3 under high-pressure and low-temperature regimes using high-pressure Raman spectroscopy and DFT calculations. Our findings demonstrate that the Néel temperature is significantly elevated by pressure, rising from 78 K at ambient pressure to 240 K at 20.2 GPa. The concomitant blueshift of a possible two-magnon-related magnetic Raman feature is consistent with the substantial enhancement of magnetic exchange under compression. This enhancement is closely linked to a pressure-induced layer- sliding transition occurring at approximately 5 GPa, which triggers a magnetic reconfiguration from interlayer ferromagnetic to interlayer antiferromagnetic coupling. These insights not only clarify the complex behavior of MnPS3 under extreme conditions but also advance our understanding of two-dimensional magnetic materials, thereby enriching the broader field of condensed matter physics.
